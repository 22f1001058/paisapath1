All nine journey maps came out the same shape, and they hit bottom in the same place.

Everyone stalls at the same point

Canvas 1 · 9 customer journey maps03 / 07
Problems uncovered · multi-why → root causes

Four gaps generate every symptom we observed

RC·1

Guidance gap

No beginner-friendly, contextual help at the transition to independence. Education is generic and jargon-first; professional advice is expensive.

RC·2

Structure gap

No planning tuned to income, goals and life stage. Our nine users needed four different budgeting approaches between them.

RC·3

Confidence gap

No way to see the reasoning behind a suggestion, and no safe way to build judgement before real money is at stake. So the decision gets put off.

RC·4

Trust gap

Data practices and commercial incentives are unclear. People cannot check whether the guidance is impartial, so they fall back on family advice.

What we kept coming back to: these users already have plenty of information. What they are short of is confidence. And trust decides whether they open the app at all.

Root causes → features · Canvas 205 / 07
RC·4 · Trust gap · the sharpest thing RD1 asked us

“Won't AI become one more conflicting advisor?”

It cannot, because it never gets an opinion. That is built into the architecture.

What we did not build

  • Another voice competing with parents, YouTube and ChatGPT
  • Product picks out of a black box the user cannot check
  • Anything tuned for engagement or commission

What we built instead

  • Deterministic rules produce every rupee on screen
  • The model gets the finished figures and puts them into plain English. It is never asked to work one out
  • Every recommendation shows its reasoning, its risk and two alternatives
Why it holds
The maths lives in one file that contains no AI at all, so you can check any figure yourself. When the AI is unreachable every screen still works. A Trust Centre logs each AI call and what got sent. It weighs up the conflicting advice for the user, and has none of its own to add.
