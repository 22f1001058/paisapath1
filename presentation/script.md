## [→] Slide 2 · Who we talked to · 0:08 to 0:46

> We interviewed nine young Indians at three different stages of handling money.
>
> Riya is nineteen and lives on an allowance. Her money disappears in small UPI payments she can't
> account for later.
>
> Samik is twenty-three and just started earning. He wants to be sensible with it. He has no idea
> whether he's getting it right.
>
> Anuj is also twenty-three and already invests. He still doesn't know if his allocation makes sense.
>
> What surprised us was that the problem didn't go away as people got more experienced. It just
> looked different.
>
> All nine had tried to track their spending and given up. All nine brought up trust or privacy on
> their own, before we asked about it. Three had already lost money. And not one of them could
> explain how to divide up a salary.

*Pause before "what surprised us". That line is the point of the whole talk.*
*Behind schedule? Drop the three names and say "a student, a first earner, an early investor". Saves 8 seconds.*

## [→] Slide 3 · Where it breaks · 0:46 to 1:13

> We mapped every person's journey, and they all came out the same shape.
>
> Everyone got stuck at the same point: comparing the options. They could follow the concepts fine.
> Choosing between a PPF, a fixed deposit and a mutual fund is where they stalled.
>
> The uncertainty got worse when their parents, YouTube and ChatGPT all gave different answers.
>
> And the lowest point on every map was doing nothing at all. "I'll decide later." The money sits in
> the account and the confidence never builds.

*Point at the red dip when you get to "the lowest point".*

## [→] Slide 4 · What's underneath · 1:13 to 1:42

> We ran a multi-why analysis on those symptoms and landed on four gaps.
>
> There's no guidance written for beginners at the moment they start earning.
>
> There's no structure that fits someone's actual life stage. Our nine users needed four different
> budgeting approaches between them.
>
> There's no way to see the reasoning behind a suggestion, so people put the decision off.
>
> And data practices are unclear, so people fall back on whatever their family tells them.
>
> The thing we kept coming back to is this. These users already have plenty of information. What
> they're short of is confidence.

*Land the last two sentences slowly. They're why we built a mentor and not another tracker.*

---

## [→] Slide 5 · What we built · 1:42 to 2:29

> Each feature answers one of those gaps.
>
> For structure, there's a questionnaire that works out your profile, then splits your salary across
> essentials, an emergency fund, investments and spending. You drag any of it until it matches your life.
>
> Also for structure, all your accounts sit on one page and your expenses sort themselves. You never
> type an expense in.
>
> For confidence, there's a what-if simulator. You type the question the way you'd say it out loud.
> "What if I invest five thousand a month instead of spending it?" It shows you both futures side by
> side before you commit to either one.
>
> For guidance, we added nudges that turn up when they're useful. Move money on the day your salary
> lands. Check the subscription that's crept up. Raise your investment after a pay rise.
>
> And the explanations live inside the app, right where you're making the decision.

*Fastest stretch. Keep the rhythm going and let the slide carry the detail.*

---

## [→] Slide 6 · Why the AI can't make it worse · 2:29 to 2:55

> In the last round, someone asked whether our AI would just become one more voice handing out
> conflicting advice.
>
> It can't, because it never gets an opinion. All the arithmetic is done by fixed rules we wrote,
> and every rupee on the screen comes out of those. The model only gets handed the finished figures
> and asked to put them into plain English.
>
> That's why you can check any number yourself, and why the app still works when the AI is
> unavailable. It weighs up the conflicting advice for you. It has none of its own to add.

*Our strongest slide. Slow down here. This answers the sharpest thing anyone has asked us.*

## If someone asks what's actually built

Working now: the dashboard, automatic categorisation, the onboarding questionnaire and profiling,
the budget generator, goal planner, simulator, nudges, in-context lessons, Trust Centre and mentor chat.

Not built: the moderated peer community. We left it out on purpose. Letting strangers hand out money
tips would undercut the trust model everything else depends on.

Also left for later, carried over from RD2: taxes, choosing insurance, credit score tools, and the
SEBI adviser marketplace.
